The seas

The world of Runners, as planned. The game is in development and details will change.

Thirteen named seas, each with one kind of danger.

The Melt

The old Arctic. The pack is gone, so there is a way over the top of the world from the Atlantic to the Pacific.

What it looks like
Grey-white water, fog, bergs the size of rigs, the midnight sun.
Why go
The only route between the oceans that pays no pirate toll. Russia's water at one end, the Ice Gate at the other.
The danger
Drift ice. The pack has melted, but what is left drifts as growlers and bergs in fog. Hull damage at speed.

See it on the map

The Fog Banks

The North Pacific from Kamchatka to Alaska.

What it looks like
Flat cold sea under fog that does not lift. Rigs appear at fifty metres.
Why go
The quiet way to the Ice Gate. The Juneau crew, and the best place in the world to not be seen.
The danger
Fog banks. Days of fog. You see nothing, and nothing sees you.

See it on the map

The Quiet Coast

ARI's home sea off drowned California. Few rigs, on purpose: this is fishing and salvage water.

What it looks like
Clear blue water, fishing fleets, ARI cutters on the horizon.
Why go
Home. Safe work that pays little: fishing escorts, salvage in drowned San Diego.
The danger
Patrolled. ARI cutters answer a distress call. The only danger is the weather.

See it on the map

The Empty Quarter

The central and south-east Pacific. Weeks of open ocean without one place to refuel.

What it looks like
Indigo deep water and nothing else. No rigs, no birds, no radio.
Why go
The straight line from home to Asia. Midway sits alone in the middle of it.
The danger
No fuel. Deep ocean with no rig in reach. Run dry here and the tow costs more than the cargo.

See it on the map

The Narrow Seas

The Gulf, the Caribbean and the Cut through drowned Central America.

What it looks like
Crowded green water, islands, rigs in sight of each other, smoke.
Why go
The richest trade in the world: Breakwater, the Federation, the Last Resort and Norfolk all within a few sectors, and the Tollmen across the door.
The danger
Pirate water. A crew or a big faction owns this sea. Pay the toll, or run it and be hunted.

See it on the map

The Grey Atlantic

The North Atlantic and the seas of northern Europe. Three navies watch each other across it.

What it looks like
Steel-grey swell, rain, warships.
Why go
Navy contracts, navy ships, and the smuggling that comes with rivals who will not trade with each other.
The danger
Navy patrols. Cutters stop and board you. Standing decides the tariff, the inspection and whether you may enter at all.

See it on the map

The Long Fetch

The South Atlantic between Africa and Brazil.

What it looks like
Long slow swell from the south, empty sky.
Why go
The back road from the Atlantic to the Cape and the Indian Ocean. Dead Rio and drowned Lagos for salvage.
The danger
No fuel. Deep ocean with no rig in reach. Run dry here and the tow costs more than the cargo.

See it on the map

The Choked Sea

What is left of the Mediterranean and the flooded land east of it.

What it looks like
Brown shallow water over drowned coast, wrecks stacked in the channels.
Why go
The Suez Cut: the short way from Europe to the Gulf, held by the Driftmark.
The danger
Drowned ruins. A drowned city under the keel: tower tops, cables, streets at low water. Deep hulls ground.

See it on the map

The Monsoon Sea

The Indian Ocean and the Gulf. Calm for half the year and closed for the other half.

What it looks like
Amber dust haze off the land, then black cyclone weather.
Why go
Gulf crude from Iran, which half the world wants. Dead Mumbai.
The danger
Storm belt. Gales and cyclones. Small hulls swamp outside rig shelter.

See it on the map

The Drowned Coast

The South China Sea. More drowned cities than anywhere else, and China's fleet at Sanya.

What it looks like
Murky water over cities: Hong Kong's towers standing out of the sea.
Why go
The big shipyards, and the richest city salvage in the world.
The danger
Drowned ruins. A drowned city under the keel: tower tops, cables, streets at low water. Deep hulls ground.

See it on the map

The Ash Sea

The seas round Japan, Korea and north China, where the war hit hardest.

What it looks like
Yellow-grey sky, dead coast, four closed radiation sectors side by side.
Why go
Dead navy ports full of electronics nobody has stripped. Saltrot's water.
The danger
Fallout drift. Beside a closed radiation zone. The danger is that nobody will tow you out, ports turn away a ship that strayed, and the closed water is one wrong turn away.

See it on the map

The Coral Graves

The seas between Indonesia and Australia.

What it looks like
Bright shallow reef water, wrecks on every reef, cyclone seasons.
Why go
More wrecks than anywhere else. Dead Sydney.
The danger
Storm belt. Gales and cyclones. Small hulls swamp outside rig shelter.

See it on the map

The Roaring Belt

The Southern Ocean. Gales all year.

What it looks like
Huge grey seas, spindrift, no land round the whole world.
Why go
The Horn, the free way round the Americas. And Dex's Wake, which nobody goes to.
The danger
Storm belt. Gales and cyclones. Small hulls swamp outside rig shelter.
